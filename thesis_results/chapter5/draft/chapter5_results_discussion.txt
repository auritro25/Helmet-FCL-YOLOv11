CHAPTER 5
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION


5.1 Experimental Setup

This section presents the experimental evaluation of the proposed federated helmet detection framework. The experiments were conducted using a YOLO11s-based object detection model enhanced with supervised contrastive learning (SupCon). The proposed framework was evaluated under centralized and federated learning settings.

The dataset consisted of three object categories:
(1) rider,
(2) helmet,
(3) no helmet.

The federated experiments were performed using three distributed clients. Two federated aggregation strategies were investigated: Federated Averaging (FedAvg) and Federated Proximal (FedProx).

The performance was evaluated using Precision, Recall, mAP50, and mAP50-95 metrics.


5.2 Evaluation Metrics

Precision measures the proportion of correctly detected objects among all predicted detections. Higher precision indicates fewer false positive detections.

Recall measures the ability of the model to identify all relevant objects in the dataset. Higher recall indicates fewer missed detections.

mAP50 represents the mean Average Precision at an Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold of 0.50.

mAP50-95 evaluates detection performance across multiple IoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.95 and provides a stricter assessment of localization quality.


5.3 Ablation Study

The ablation study investigates the contribution of different components in the proposed framework. Three configurations were evaluated:

A1: SupCon YOLO11s centralized model

A2: SupCon YOLO11s with FedAvg aggregation

A3: SupCon YOLO11s with FedProx aggregation


5.3.1 Effect of SupCon Feature Learning

The centralized SupCon YOLO11s model achieved:

Precision: 0.852
Recall: 0.881
mAP50: 0.918
mAP50-95: 0.604

The results demonstrate the effectiveness of contrastive feature learning for improving feature representation and object localization.


5.3.2 Effect of Federated Training

FedAvg and FedProx were evaluated under distributed client training. The federated models maintained comparable performance while enabling decentralized training without requiring centralized data storage.


5.4 FedAvg Performance Analysis

The final FedAvg model after 10 communication rounds achieved:

Precision: 0.8531
Recall: 0.8883
mAP50: 0.9018
mAP50-95: 0.5861

The results indicate that iterative aggregation allowed the model to improve detection capability across distributed clients. The recall value demonstrates the ability of the federated model to identify helmet-related objects effectively.


5.5 FedProx Performance Analysis

The preliminary FedProx evaluation achieved:

Precision: 0.8502
Recall: 0.8779
mAP50: 0.9112
mAP50-95: 0.5970

FedProx introduced a proximal regularization mechanism to reduce deviation from the global model during local updates. The obtained results indicate stable performance under distributed optimization.


5.6 Comparative Analysis

The comparison between FedAvg and FedProx demonstrates different optimization behaviours.

FedAvg achieved slightly higher precision and recall, while FedProx achieved higher mAP50 and mAP50-95 values in the evaluated setting.

These differences indicate that aggregation strategy influences model convergence and localization performance.


5.7 Qualitative Detection Results

Visual prediction results are presented to demonstrate detection behaviour under different scenarios, including helmet detection, no-helmet detection, multiple riders, and challenging image conditions.


5.8 Limitations and Future Work

The current study has several limitations:

1. Limited number of federated clients.
2. Limited evaluation dataset.
3. Computational constraints during federated experimentation.
4. Lack of deployment on real edge devices.

Future work may investigate:
- larger federated networks,
- adaptive FedProx parameters,
- privacy-preserving mechanisms,
- real-time edge deployment.
